Nationals Sign Eric Crippen to Replace a .563 OPS at Shortstop

Crippen's rookie October ended with Washington beating his Rays in a five-game World Series.

By Teo Vasquez · January 9, 2059

The Washington Nationals have signed shortstop Eric Crippen away from Tampa Bay for seven years and $245 million. The money goes to the one spot where their lineup came up empty last season.

Washington spent $233.7 million on players in 2058, more than any club in the league, and paid the luxury tax for it. The return was an 80-82 record, third place and no postseason. That ended a run of playoff trips that began in 2044.

Shortstop was the hole. Luke Christensen played there most, and in 462 plate appearances he hit six home runs with a .275 on-base percentage and a .563 OPS. Crippen, in the worst of his last three seasons, finished 165 points of OPS and 13 home runs ahead of him.

Drafted eighth overall out of an Evansville, Ind., high school in 2049, Crippen gave the Rays 33.8 WAR across seven big-league seasons. Tampa Bay reached the postseason in every one of them. His best year was 2057, when he hit .310 with a .368 OBP and 23 home runs and won the Silver Slugger at shortstop.

His 11.7 zone rating last season ranked second among the 12 American League shortstops who played half their club's innings there. The glove travels with the bat.

Crippen fell to .245/.314/.414 in 2058, and only 115 of his 133 games came at shortstop. That decline is the part Washington has to accept on faith. In October he hit .296 with a .500 slugging percentage over 15 games as the Rays lost the World Series in seven.

In 2053, Crippen's rookie season, the Nationals beat his Rays 4-1 in the World Series for the second of back-to-back titles. Washington knows him from the other dugout.

At $35 million a season, Crippen is now Washington's highest-paid player, ahead of second baseman Gus Garcia at $30 million. The deal ranks 10th in the league by average annual value among 297 contracts in force. Crippen can opt out of the deal after the 2062 season.

Of the $103.5 million Washington has guaranteed for 2060 so far, $35 million belongs to the shortstop.
