Red Sox Bet One Guaranteed Year on Jorge Vasquez's October Résumé

The 37-year-old carried a 6.37 ERA into July after spring biceps tendinitis, then posted a 2.92 the rest of the way.

By Teo Vasquez · February 2, 2059

The Boston Red Sox have made 15 postseason trips since 2016 without winning a title. They have now signed the pitcher who started 31 playoff games for Washington and won two championships there.

Jorge Vasquez's three-year, $115.6 million deal reads bigger than it is. Only 2059 is guaranteed, at $38.8 million. The club holds options on 2060 and 2061, and the $15 million buyout on the first brings the guaranteed money to $53.8 million. Boston is paying for one October and keeping the right to decide on the rest, which is the right size of bet on a 37-year-old.

Vasquez spent 15 seasons in Washington's rotation after the Nationals took him in the third round in 2039, and he leaves with 64.4 WAR there. His club reached the postseason in 14 of those 15 years. The one it missed was last year.

Biceps tendinitis, announced March 20 with a six-week estimate, cost him the opening weeks. Through his first nine starts he had a 6.37 ERA and allowed 1.84 home runs per nine innings. From July 1 on he made 16 starts with a 2.92 ERA. Over that stretch he struck out 91 in 98 2/3 innings. He finished at 25 starts, his fewest as a big-leaguer.

The second half looked like the pitcher who won the Cy Young in 2054, 2055 and 2057. The first half looked like a man near the end. Boston's guarantee covers exactly the year it takes to learn which one it signed.

Boston's rotation finished 13th of 30 clubs in ERA at 3.83 last season. No Red Sox starter reached 200 innings, with Gabe Garcia's 192 the most. The club still won the division at 93-69 and lost the League Championship Series to Tampa Bay in six games.

Vasquez threw at least 207 innings in every full season from 2050 through 2057. In October he has been sharper still. Across his playoff career he has a 3.21 ERA in 202 innings, against 3.78 over 3,018 1/3 innings in the regular season.

His salary now tops the club's 2059 list, ahead of Matt Ballard's $30 million. With Vasquez included, Boston carries $246.9 million in guaranteed 2059 money, the most in BLM, and it paid luxury tax last season. The deal's average annual value ranks eighth among the 312 big-league contracts in force.

Ballard, Dan Daniels and Josh Wood are all guaranteed through 2059 with club options for 2060, and Vasquez's deal takes the same shape. The structure is a Boston habit, and it puts all four 2060 decisions on next winter's desk.

The club's control has a limit even if the summer pitcher is the real one. Vasquez holds an opt-out after the 2060 season, so a strong 2059 earns Boston no more than one further year of him before he can test the market again.
